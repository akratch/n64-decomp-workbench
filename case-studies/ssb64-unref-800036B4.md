# Case study: SSB64 `unref_800036B4` — how to publish an impossibility proof and be wrong in a day

**Who this is for:** you have run a line-number sweep (field guide lever 23,
`probe-lines`) on a `schedule-mismatch`, found a clean acceptance plateau, and
concluded that no natural source layout can reach it — that the function needs
`#line`, or a different preprocessor, or nothing at all. Read this before you
write that down anywhere a stranger can read it. The conclusion is probably
true of the variants you built and false of the language.

**What this campaign was:** `unref_800036B4`, the VPK0/HAL decompression
decoder in Super Smash Bros. 64 (US) `sys/dma`. 339 instructions, IDO 7.1
`-O2 -mips2`, worked entirely in decomp.me form (preset 49) against an exported
scratch. It went from decomp.me score 290 to a byte-identical `.text` in a day,
through four distinct mechanisms, and the last of the four came from a stranger
who read the writeup and disproved its central claim in an afternoon. That last
step is why this case study exists; the first three are the setup.

---

## Recognize these two situations first

| Symptom | Verdict / signature | Section |
|---|---|---|
| A small residue of pure adjacent transpositions on a `-g0` build, register allocation identical, and a statement whose operand the target emits *before* a statement written above it | `schedule-mismatch`, `structural=0 register=0`, two swapped pairs | [The residue](#the-residue-four-words-two-independent-pairs) |
| A line-number sweep gives a sharp plateau you cannot reach with one statement per physical line | plateau boundaries that bracket lines *inside* a block | [The correction](#the-correction-non-decreasing-is-not-strictly-increasing) |

---

## 290 → 120: the missing statement, found by verdict and not by reading

The starting scratch (`GEmJz`, 290/33900) read `structure-mismatch` with 338
instructions against the target's 339 and `aligned_total=32`. One instruction
short, thirty-two words wrong: that ratio is the signature of a *missing
value*, not of thirty-two wrong expressions.

The missing instruction was `move t0,a1` — the function writes its output
through a byte cursor that the decompilation had folded into the output
pointer parameter. Splitting `bytecsr` back out as its own local added the one
instruction and took the verdict to `schedule-mismatch` with `aligned_total=3`
(scratch `CzZuv`, 120/33900). One local, 31 downstream sites.

Nothing about this step is novel; it is here because it is the part that
verdict classification does for you. `structure-mismatch` with a
one-instruction size gap says *find the value that has no home*, and the
allocator census (field guide lever 6) says which one.

---

## The residue: four words, two independent pairs

From 120 the entire remaining diff was four words inside one six-instruction
window, `0x64..0x70`:

```text
row  target                    | candidate
 25  srl  t8,t7,0x18           | addiu a0,t1,10
 26  addiu a0,t1,10            | srl  t8,t7,0x18
 27  addiu a2,sp,292           | move t0,a1
 28  move t0,a1                | addiu a2,sp,292
```

Two independent adjacent transpositions. `structural=0 register=0 schedule=3`,
the temp-register FIFO identical over all 83 slots, no register web miscolored.
This is the cleanest possible schedule verdict: the allocator agrees with the
target completely and the emission order does not.

The cheap levers were discharged first and all came back empty:

- **Flag and context parity (lever 4).** The local IDO 7.1 build reproduced
  decomp.me's own `current.o` `.text` byte for byte for *both* scratches. The
  harness was not the variable.
- **`context lint` (lever 24).** Clean.
- **The `-g0` probe (lever 3).** Vacuous, and provably so: `-g0` is already the
  default here, and `-g3` produces byte-identical `.text`. Debug records do not
  own this ordering in either direction.

That leaves lever 23. And the source mapping was unambiguous:
`addiu a2,sp,292` is `var_s3 = sp14C;` and `move t0,a1` is
`bytecsr = out_buf;` — **the target emits a textually later statement first.**

---

## 4 → 0: the `#line` campaign, and a plateau that looked like a proof

128 variant directories, 119 distinct sources. The relevant families:

| family | n | what it did |
|---|---|---|
| line merges, comma fusions, blank-line insertion and deletion, whole-block compression | 41 | most produced a `.text` bit-identical to the baseline, four moved it sideways or worse; **none matched** |
| natural statement moves (`var_s3` hoisted up, three placements) | 3 | fixed one pair, left the other — **2 words** |
| `var_s3` up *and* `sp134` hoisted into the `if` block (semantics changed) | 1 | 22 — worse |
| `#line` on one statement | 15 | half the residue collapses — the lever is real |
| `#line` on two statements, 2-D grid plus boundary bisection | 65 | **0** |

The winning edit moved nothing and rewrote no expression:

```c
#line 13284
    var_s3 = sp14C;
#line 13290
    sp134 = (temp_s2 << (0x18 - temp_s1)) >> 0x18;
#line 13298
    i = 0;
```

with bisected, rectangular acceptance windows: `L(var_s3) ≤ 13284` (13284 is
`bytecsr = out_buf;`'s own line) and `L(sp134) ∈ [13285, 13292]` (13292 is the
last statement *inside* the preceding `if` block). Byte-identical `.text`,
1360 bytes.

And then the campaign wrote down what it thought it had proved:

> A natural layout can only ever assign line numbers that are non-decreasing
> along statement order. The target requires a statement that appears *after*
> `bytecsr = out_buf;`, and after an entire `if` block, to carry a line number
> at or *before* `bytecsr`'s. That is a decreasing line number along statement
> order. It cannot be expressed by any arrangement of newlines, in the same way
> that no arrangement of the digits of a number can make it both odd and even.
> `#line` is the only source form that reaches the target.

Forty-six no-`#line` variants and a bisected plateau behind it. It reads like
evidence. It is evidence — for a much smaller claim than the one it was
attached to.

---

## The correction: non-decreasing is not strictly increasing

decomp.me user **inspectredc** read the writeup and posted scratch `zPJCY` the
same day: score 0, same context pane, **no `#line` anywhere**. Verified against
the same local harness — `.text` sha1 identical, `diff_words=0`.

Their diff against the score-120 source is three edits, two of which matter:

```c
    var_s3 = sp14C;               /* 1. hoisted to the top of the body */
    temp_s1 = 0;
    ...
    bytecsr = out_buf;

    if (temp_s1 <= 0)                                            \
    {                                                            \
        temp_s1 -= 0x8;                                          \
        temp_s2 <<= 0x10, temp_s2 |= *(csr++), temp_s1 += 0x10;  \
    }                                                            \
    sp134 = (temp_s2 << (0x18 - temp_s1)) >> 0x18;    /* 2. spliced */
```

1. **A legal statement hoist.** `var_s3 = sp14C;` moves above
   `bytecsr = out_buf;`. This is the family the campaign scored at 2 words and
   filed as "partial, strictly dominated by the `#line` form, dead end".
2. **A backslash splice covering the closing brace.** Every physical line of the
   `if` block ends in `\` — *including* the `}` — so the block and the statement
   after it are one **logical** line.

`cfe` numbers statements by logical line, not physical line. So `sp134` receives
the line number of the `if` head: a number from *inside* the block. The
`cc -K` listing says it without inference — the four instructions that compute
`sp134` sit under `.loc 2 13302` spliced, and under `.loc 2 13307` unspliced,
with no other difference in that window.

**The false step was one word.** "Non-decreasing" is correct. "So every natural
layout yields the same relative ordering key" is not, because non-decreasing
permits **ties**, and ties are free: several statements on one physical line, or
several physical lines joined with `\`. Splicing a block's `}` and the following
statement back to the block's first line is exactly how a statement below a
block acquires a number from inside it.

Worse, the campaign's own ledger already contained the corollary — "merging two
statements onto one line *is* a real perturbation, because equal line numbers
break the strict monotonicity" — filed as a curiosity two paragraphs below the
impossibility claim it contradicted.

## The ablation: which tie, and where

Twenty-six variants against the same harness pin both boundaries. Line numbers
are `code.c` lines of `zPJCY`: `bytecsr = out_buf;` is 90, the `if` head 92,
`{` 93, body 94 and 95, `}` 96, `sp134` 97 when unspliced.

| variant | edit | diff_words |
|---|---|---|
| `zPJCY` verbatim | — | **0** |
| every trailing `\` removed, hoist kept | no tie | 2 — `srl`/`addiu a0` |
| hoist reverted, splices kept | no hoist | 49 |
| splice head…`}` but not onto `sp134`'s line | tie stops short | 2 |
| tie `sp134` to the `}` line only | `L(sp134)` = 96 | 2 |
| the same tie written as `} sp134 = …;`, no backslash | `L(sp134)` = 96 | 2 |
| whole block **and** `sp134` on one physical line, no backslashes | `L(sp134)` = 92 | **0** |
| splice start walked 92 → 95 | `L(sp134)` = 92, 93, 94, 95 | **0** ×4 |
| splice start on the blank line above the block | `L(sp134)` = 91 | **0** |
| splice start on the `bytecsr` line | `L(sp134)` = `L(bytecsr)` | 2 |
| `var_s3` on the *same physical line* as `bytecsr` | tie, no hoist | **0** |
| `var_s3` on its own line one below `bytecsr` | +1 | 2 — `addiu a2`/`move t0` |
| comment header of 0, 1, 15, 30 lines | uniform shift | **0** ×4 |

Three things fall out:

- **The two levers are one per pair.** The tie fixes rows 25–26, the hoist fixes
  rows 27–28. Neither alone reaches 0.
- **Backslash splicing and same-physical-line placement are the same lever.**
  They produce identical objects at every tie point tested. The backslash form
  is only more readable.
- **The plateaus are relative, not absolute.** Uniform shifts of the whole file
  are inert, so what the scheduler compares is line numbers against each other.
  Restated, the acceptance window is `L(var_s3) ≤ L(bytecsr)` and
  `L(bytecsr) < L(sp134) ≤ L(last statement inside the block)` — which maps 1:1
  onto the absolute window the `#line` grid bisected. Every measurement
  survived. Only the sentence about what they implied did not.

## The near miss, in the campaign's own table

Both halves of `zPJCY` were already on disk, in the same experiment matrix,
each written off for a different bad reason:

| variant that existed | verdict recorded | what it actually was |
|---|---|---|
| the whole `if` block merged onto one line | "inert" | half the answer — it stopped one statement short of `sp134` |
| `var_s3` moved up | "2 words, partial, strictly dominated, dead end" | the other half |

Composing them scores 2. Moving `sp134` onto that same merged line scores **0**.
Two edits, from variants a 128-variant campaign had already built and scored.

---

## Meta-lessons

1. **An impossibility claim is a claim about your search space until you write
   its scope next to it.** The defensible sentence was always *"under the
   statement order of this file, one statement per physical line, the 41 layouts
   tried do not reach the target"* — which is still true, and which nobody would
   have needed to falsify. Dropping two qualifiers turned a measurement into a
   theorem, and a theorem is the kind of thing a stranger can disprove in an
   afternoon. Bisections measure a plateau *under the layout you swept*.
2. **A statement move that fixes one of two residual sites is a new baseline,
   not a dead end.** "Partial, strictly dominated" compares the move against a
   shortcut you already have; it says nothing about the move. The rule that
   would have found this: when a family fixes one pair and leaves another,
   re-run every layout lever *on top of* it before dropping it. Domination by a
   directive is a fact about the directive.
3. **"Lever inert" often means "lever applied to the wrong span".** The block
   merge covered five lines. The target needed six. One line of span was the
   entire difference between "inert" and "matched", and nothing in the null
   result distinguished the two.
4. **Publish the harness, not just the answer.** Every variant here was a
   `code.c` plus two symlinks and a two-line build; that is why a stranger's
   scratch could be verified locally in one command, and why the ablation that
   explains it took twenty minutes rather than a day. The falsification was
   cheap to accept *because* the campaign was reproducible. A campaign that only
   publishes conclusions cannot be corrected — it can only be doubted.
